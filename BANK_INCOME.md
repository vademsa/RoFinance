# Bank-only receipts

In the transaction form, select **Income → Income destination → Receive in bank account without jar allocation**, then choose a saved account. This records money already received; it does not contact a bank or initiate a real transfer. To allocate a previously recorded receipt later, edit that transaction and change its destination to jars instead of recording the same receipt twice.

- Bank-only receipts have `type: income`, `bankAccountId`, an empty `jarId`, and no jar allocations. They cannot be expenses or transfers. The backend checks the account against the owner's validated bank-account list and derives the bank identity from that account.
- `monthlyIncome` remains the income used for jar allocation. Current analytics add bank-only receipts once. Completed summaries include them in `income` and track the included amount in `unallocatedIncome`, so rebuilding a summary does not count them twice.
- Account balances combine remaining linked-jar funds, `unallocatedOpeningBalance`, and all bank-only receipts for that account across cycles. The opening amount is persisted once for legacy standalone accounts. Manual reconciliation adjusts it; it may be negative as a reconciliation offset. Repeated synchronisation/reloads do not compound receipts.
- Editing, moving between accounts/cycles, allocating, or deleting a receipt recalculates balances from the ledger. Backdated bank-only receipts do not need a historical jar snapshot and do not replay or modify historical jar budgets. An account referenced by a receipt cannot be deleted while that receipt remains.
- Bank-only funds remain in their original account when jars are changed or unlinked. Bank identities use saved account IDs, so editing account details does not lose the receipt linkage.
- Amount suggestions use the entered value ×100, ×1,000, ×10,000, and ×100,000. Thus `2` suggests `200`, `2,000`, `20,000`, `200,000`, while `20` suggests `2,000`, `20,000`, `200,000`, `2,000,000`. Suggestions beyond the supported monetary limit are omitted; hidden-amount mode hides them.
